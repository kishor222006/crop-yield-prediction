Here's the practical difference for users:

---

### Yield Prediction — The "What is" (Forecast)

**User walks in thinking:**  
*"My crop is maize, it's Kharif season, and right now it's 32°C with 60% rain and 70% humidity. What will my yield be?"*

**What they get:**  
A single number: **"Your predicted yield is 5.2 t/ha with a Low risk score (22/100)"**

**Why it matters:**  
- Farmers/agents need to know if the current conditions will produce a good harvest
- Helps with planning harvest logistics, storage, and market commitments
- Shows how close they are to the crop's potential yield

---

### What If Simulator — The "What if" (Decision Support)

**User walks in thinking:**  
*"The forecast says rain will drop 30% next month. Should I irrigate? Will my yield drop enough to justify the cost?"*

**What they get:**  
Side-by-side comparison:
- **Baseline:** 5.2 t/ha, 22/100 risk
- **Scenario (−30% rain):** 4.3 t/ha, 48/100 risk  
- **Delta:** −17% yield, +26 risk points

**Why it matters:**  
- Answers economic questions: *"Is the 17% yield loss worth spending ₹X on irrigation?"*
- Enables proactive decisions: *"If temperature rises +3°C (heatwave), my risk jumps from 22→67 — better adjust planting schedule now"*
- Shows trade-offs before committing to expensive actions

---

### Real-world analogy

- **Yield Prediction** = Your car's GPS telling you *"Estimated arrival: 3:45 PM"*  
- **What If Simulator** = *"What if there's a traffic jam? 3:45→4:20. What if I take the highway instead? 3:35. Which route should I choose?"*

The simulator doesn't predict the future — it helps users **stress-test their decisions** against different weather outcomes.
